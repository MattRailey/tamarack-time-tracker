Glulam beam sizing

Preliminary uniform-load beam check. Gives a starting section — not a substitute for a stamped calculation.

Assumes simple span, uniform load. Width is fixed by your selection above — the calculator solves only for the required depth at that width; it will not swap you to a different width to save material. Default is 5-1/2", Tamarack's typical beam width. Note two separate glulam width families exist: 5-1/2"/7-1/2"/9-1/2" are the Southern Pine series, manufactured net-width to match dimension lumber (flush with a 2x6/2x8/2x10 wall or post); 3-1/8"/5-1/8"/6-3/4"/8-3/4" are the Western species (Douglas Fir) series, which do NOT match dimension lumber widths. Confirm which series your supplier stocks before specifying. Verify bearing, lateral-torsional buckling (CL), and connection design separately.

Sx and I by width and depth, generated on the fly. Covers both the Southern Pine series (5-1/2", 7-1/2", 9-1/2" — matches dimension lumber width) and the Western/DF series (3-1/8", 5-1/8", 6-3/4", 8-3/4" — does not match dimension lumber).

Site design data (by ZIP)

Rough regional planning values for Oregon, Washington, and Idaho sites, to get you in the ballpark before an EOR pulls exact site-specific hazard data.

Values are coarse 3-digit-ZIP regional estimates, not a site-specific hazard report. Ground snow load in the Cascades/Rockies foothills and the eastern Idaho/Yellowstone corridor is strongly elevation- and fault-proximity-dependent — always confirm snow against the state/county-adopted snow load map (SEAO for Oregon; local jurisdiction maps for WA and ID) and confirm wind/seismic against the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool (asce7hazardtool.online) before finalizing a design. Coverage: Oregon (970–979), Washington (980–986, 988–994), and Idaho (832–838) 3-digit ZIP prefixes. Elevations are regional-default (largest city/town in that prefix) — always enter the actual site elevation above when you know it, since it drives the snow load estimate directly.

Pages align by the page NUMBERS you set above, not by title block or sheet number — if a sheet was inserted or removed mid-set, the two page numbers will drift out of alignment further into the set. Step pages independently on each side to re-sync. On render resolution: a literal 600 DPI render of a full 24"x36"+ sheet is roughly 20,000+ pixels wide — no browser canvas or in-browser pixel diff can hold or process that without crashing the tab. "Maximum" renders at 4000px, which is the highest width this tool can diff at interactively; on a 30"-wide sheet that's about 130 DPI on-screen, and combined with scroll-wheel zoom (up to 6x) gives you well beyond 600 DPI of effective magnification once you're zoomed into a detail — the rendered pixels are just being magnified further, same as zooming into any raster image. For true pixel-level, print-resolution inspection of one specific area, download the current view as PNG and open it in an image viewer at 100%.

Rebar shape designer

Pick a typical bar shape, set the bar size and leg dimensions, and export it as a masking-region outline to drop straight into a Revit drafting view. No fill, Medium Lines border, no callouts — matches how you draw these by hand. The preview IS the check: if a shape looks wrong here, it'll be wrong in Revit too, so flag it before using it on a real set.

Bend geometry follows ACI 318 Ch. 25 minimum bend diameters (6db for #3–#8, 8db for #9–#11, 4db for stirrup/tie bends #3–#5) and standard hook extensions — a reasonable field default, not a substitute for checking against the CRSI Manual of Standard Practice or the project's own bar list requirements before fabrication.
